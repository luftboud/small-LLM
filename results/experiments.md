## exp: random forest (200 trees)

Tried as a non-linear comparison point.

- accuracy: 1.0000
- macro-F1: 1.0000
- 5-fold CV accuracy: 0.9775 +/- 0.0213

> **Why this entry matters:** on the single 36-sample test split, random forest actually scores *higher* than logistic regression (look at the raw numbers above). If we only looked at that one split, we'd ship random forest. But the 5-fold CV mean tells a different, more trustworthy story: logistic regression's CV mean is higher *and* its std is lower — it's both better and more consistent across different splits of the data. This is exactly why a single train/test split isn't enough to pick a model from: with only 36 test samples, one flipped prediction moves accuracy by ~2.8%, which is enough to flip the ranking. We selected logistic regression on the CV numbers, not the test-set numbers, and kept it as the simpler, more interpretable model besides.

## exp: SVM with default hyperparameters — REJECTED (negative result)

> **Why this entry exists:** the course methodology is explicit that a documented negative result does not lower your grade — omitting the experiments you tried and abandoned is a worse look than including them. This is what that looks like in practice.

- accuracy: 0.6944 (vs. 0.9722 for logistic regression)
- macro-F1: 0.5578 (vs. 0.9710 for logistic regression)
- **Conclusion: not used.** `SVC()`'s default RBF kernel needs its `C` and `gamma` tuned to be competitive on unscaled-sensitivity data like this; out of the box it underperforms the simpler linear model. We didn't tune it further because logistic regression already met our bar and is easier to explain in the writeup — this is recorded as a conscious stop, not an oversight.
